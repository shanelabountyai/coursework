# PRD: Coursework — Course Platform with a Prerequisite DAG and an Exact Gradebook

**Sample business:** "Coursework," a small professional training school (synthetic): 40 courses in six tracks with prerequisite chains, 7 certificates, 300 learners with completion histories, 16 drip-released cohorts, 4 of them graded
**Build log:** #15 Course Platform, main track. Chips: prerequisite DAG · cycle detection · derived progress · weighted grades
**Status:** Draft v1.0 — kickoff kit, oracle run twice with identical hashes (see `docs/decisions.md` §Review log)
**Learning objectives:** graph data structures in a product — prerequisites as a DAG with cycle detection on publish ("C requires B requires A", rejected with the cycle path), topological order, eligibility with a minimal missing set, shortest path to a certificate over AND/OR prerequisites; progress as derived state computed from events and an immutable catalog version, never stored; weighted-gradebook arithmetic in exact rationals rounded once; drip-released content on a calendar-day axis

**Positioning note:** the course platform itself (courses, cohorts, enrollments, content, a gradebook screen) is a known shape and is built to be competent, not to teach. The lessons are the two pure engines: nothing else in the portfolio has a graph whose edits must be refused when they close a loop, or a grade whose pass/fail can hinge on a half of a hundredth of a percent. `docs/portfolio-overlap.md` lists what is ported, from where, and what is new.

`docs/decisions.md` outranks this document wherever they differ.

## ⚠️ Scope Honesty (read first)

Learning project, synthetic data only. Every learner, completion, score and course is generated by `tools/build_fixtures.py` from a seed; names come from fixed lists and emails use `example.org`. No real school, learner or transcript is involved, and nothing here is an accredited credential or a legal record. No payments, no video hosting, no email sending, no SCORM/LTI. The eligibility, path and grade numbers in this project are *agreement with the kit's own oracle on the kit's own data*, never a claim about any institution's policy. Banner verbatim in the README.

## Problem Statement

A training school's catalog is a graph that everyone edits by hand. An administrator adds "ACC-301 now requires ACC-401" without noticing that ACC-401 already requires ACC-301, and from then on nobody can take either. A learner asks "what do I need next to finish the Analyst certificate?" and gets a different answer from each advisor, because the catalog has OR-prerequisites and the advisor picks a branch by habit. Progress bars are stored percentages that stop matching reality the day a course is added to a certificate. The gradebook is a spreadsheet with floats: a learner's 69.995 shows as 70.00, passes on one export and fails on the next, and nobody can say which rounding happened where.

Coursework treats the catalog as a versioned DAG whose publish step refuses cycles and names them; eligibility, next steps and progress are computed from completion events against the catalog version the learner enrolled under; and the gradebook is integer hundredths and exact fractions until the one place a rounding rule is stated. The builder lesson: a graph is only a data structure if the invariants are enforced at the write, and a number is only a grade if you can show the fraction.

## Goals

1. **A catalog edit that creates a cycle is rejected with the cycle path listed in a stated order.** Self-references, cycles through OR branches and two cycles in one edit are all caught (brief G3).
2. **Eligibility, next course and shortest path are deterministic and explainable:** blocked learners see the minimal missing set; the path to a certificate is the fewest courses with a stated tiebreak, exact over OR branches (G7, G8).
3. **Progress is never stored.** Course and certificate progress are derived from events and a content-hashed catalog version; `progress:verify` recomputes everything and matches the oracle (P1, V1).
4. **Grades are exact until the final rounding.** Hundredths of a percent, basis-point weights, rationals in between, half-up once; a learner on each side of the pass boundary is a golden case (S8).
5. **Drip release is day arithmetic, not clock arithmetic;** completing an unreleased item is refused (R1, R2).
6. **(Builder goal)** The TypeScript engines agree with the Python oracle on every one of 10,800 eligibility rows, 300 paths, 2,051 drip verdicts, 341 progress values and 106 grades — and say so in numbers.

## Non-Goals

- **Payments, subscriptions, seat licensing.** Enrollment is an administrative act.
- **Content hosting** (video, SCORM, LTI). A content item is a title and an offset.
- **Sending anything.** Unlock notices are queued to the outbox and never sent (D-22).
- **Transcripts as legal records, accreditation, credential verification.**
- **Minimum-grade prerequisites, co-requisites, grade replacement on retake, incompletes** (O-3; seams only).
- **Automatic re-evaluation of every learner when a catalog version is published** (O-7; explicit action).
- **A general rules engine.** Prerequisites are a closed two-level expression (D-11); schemes are a fixed shape (S1).
- **Multi-tenant billing, public API, LLM features.**

## Personas

- **Ruth, registrar** — edits the catalog, publishes versions, and needs the publish screen to tell her what breaks: the cycle, the draft course a published course would require, the 17 learners mid-course who would lose eligibility.
- **Dev, learner** — wants one answer to "can I take this" and "what do I need next" and wants the grade screen to show the fraction when the rounded number is on the line.
- **Marisol, instructor** — runs a cohort: sets the drip schedule, posts assignments, enters scores, and needs the empty-category and extra-credit rules to be the same for every learner.

## Phasing

1. **Engines + oracle agreement + eval, no writes.** `src/graph/` and `src/grades/` from the brief; `course:eval`; `progress:verify`; goldens; `docs/EVAL.md`. Fixtures are loaded read-only in memory. No Prisma models for courses, learners or grades yet.
2. **Schema, triggers, writes, proofs.** Append-only tables, publish-through-engine, item-completion-through-engine, superseding score corrections, tamper tests.
3. **UI surface.** Catalog editor with live cycle check, learner path view, gradebook per cohort, drip calendar; outbox for unlock notices if time allows (O-9).

## Risks

- **Combinatorial path search.** G8 enumerates ANY-branch combinations; a real catalog with many ORs could explode. Mitigation: depth ≤ 2 (D-11), an asserted cap of 4,096 (D-18), `cat-v1` maximum 16, and the count printed by `course:eval`.
- **Rational blow-up.** Denominators multiply across categories. Mitigation: BigInt and reduction by gcd after each operation; the `exact` column in `expected_grades.csv` is the check.
- **Policy disguised as arithmetic.** Redistribution, tie-breaks and the floor are choices. Mitigation: each is a D-item with an O-item for the alternative; the screen shows the flag that fired.
- **Sandbox cannot download Prisma engines or browsers.** Mitigation: D-4 — CI decides; the session reports the local failure verbatim.
- **Scope creep into the platform half.** Mitigation: Phase 3 is last and its task list is written only at the Phase 2 gate.
